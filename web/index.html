Method

Ice grows with accumulated freezing degree-days and melts with accumulated positive degree-hours. Total thickness at a point is

h = max(0, k·√(max(0, S − C1·ln d)) − m·P)

where S is cumulative freezing degree-days from 1 March, P is cumulative positive degree-hours over 1 March – 31 July, and d is water depth. Depth enters logarithmically, so a shallow bay freezes further than a deep basin under the same weather. Constants are fitted on 74 lakes that have measured thickness, split 80/20 by lake with a fixed seed; validation lakes are never used to choose parameters.

k1.6865growth rate
C134.38depth term, ln d with d in metres
m0.3318melt per positive degree-hour

The model clears per-month climatology on held-out lakes, but the margin is narrow and uneven: it wins in January–April and loses in November, December and May. It is, honestly, a mid-winter growth model. It under-predicts freeze-up and cannot reproduce spring melt-out.
